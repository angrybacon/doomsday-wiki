## Preamble

This appendix describes important card interactions that may not be obvious to a
newcomer. All card references are kept for historical purposes but might not be
popular or even relevant in today's competitive lists. The descriptions try to
be short and avoid gameplay aids to remain easy to parse and maintain. You'll
likely read this page only once to familiarize yourself with common interactions
in the Legacy format.

Sections are titled after the card names, alphabetically. Until a better
filtering is implemented, use your browser's search feature or the table of
contents to jump to sections.

## Your Interactions

Most of your own interactions will be intuitive and self-explanatory (i.e.
reading the card explains the card), but this is Legacy, and the card pool only
ever grows, with ever more text on new printings, so there _are_ nuances to be
aware of.

### Act on Impulse &bull; Three Wishes

:::row
Act on Impulse
Three Wishes
:::

There have been many printings of this effect, so we mention those 2 only for
historical purposes.

Exiling cards doesn't count as drawing, so you cannot lose (or win) by casting
those when 2 cards remain in your library. The exiled cards can be cast, but
their activated abilities cannot be used as if they were in your hand. For
example, _Cycling_ and _Warp_ don't work with cards in exile.

### Cavern of Souls

:::row
Cavern of Souls
:::

For :card[TO], this can name _Merfolk_ or _Wizard_. This will let you cast an
Oracle through opposing countermagic, but also through :card[Chalice of the
Void].

### Conjurer's Bauble

:::row
Conjurer's Bauble
:::

Only target a card if you actually need it restocked. Providing a target gives
your opponent something to interact with: if the target gets exiled from the
graveyard in response, the ability fizzles and you won't draw a card.

### Chromatic Sphere

:::row
Chromatic Sphere
:::

Chromatic Sphere's activated ability is no longer a mana ability as of August
7th, 2026. See [605.1a](https://yawgatog.com/resources/magic-rules/#R6051a).

Back when we still used :card[Laboratory Maniac], we would sometimes be able to
play around removal by drawing off the sphere without ever passing back priority
due to the draw effect coming off a mana ability. This is no longer possible and
activating the sphere now works like any other activated ability: you pass
priority before resolving it.

### Daze

:::row
Daze
:::

If you have a land drop available, you can use :card[Daze] to generate
additional mana of a specific color by playing an otherwise dead card and
targeting it with :card[Daze]. This costs 2 cards but could represent the third
{B} you needed to cast :card[Dd] or the second {U} to cast :card[TO].

### Doomsday

:::row
Doomsday
:::

:card[Dd] rounds up the life loss, so casting it at 1 life will result in
defeat. This also means that if you're on an odd life total, it's more
advantageous life-wise to crack your fetchland before resolving :card[Dd].

### Lake of the Dead

:::row
Lake of the Dead
:::

The Lake does not actually enter the battlefield until after a swamp is
sacrificed. Therefore, it is not possible to do any tricks like activating it in
response to its own replacement effect. You really do need 2 swamps in play for
the burst of mana.

### Lion's Eye Diamond

:::row
LED
:::

As in any Storm deck, the Diamond can be cracked in response to draw effects and
cantrips such as :card[Bs] or :card[Pnd]. Holding priority while casting these
cantrips will allow you to crack the :card[LED] and discard your hand but still
have the mana as you draw into the next card of your library. In paper you are
required to announce that you are holding priority in a timely manner. On MTGO,
you also need to maintain priority, and how to do so is documented here:
<https://www.mtgo.com/getting-started/getting-started-tips-tricks>.

### Predict

:::row
Predict
:::

This is usually meant for piles but can target the opponent as well, for
instance after a _Surveil_, against a :card[Counterbalance], or if you suspect a
_Miracle_ setup from the opponent. Do note however, that being blue, it can be
countered by :card[VoS] and you won't draw any card as a result.

### Shelldock Isle

:::row
Shelldock Isle
:::

_Hideaway_ has received a rule change and no longer lets us order the unselected
cards at the bottom of the library as of April 29th, 2022. See
[702.75a](https://yawgatog.com/resources/magic-rules/#R70275a).

The Isle has historically been played to provide an alternative win condition,
more difficult to interact with. It would usually involve :card[Em] and works by
casting the hidden spell as part of the resolution of the activated ability, at
instant speed.

### Thassa's Oracle

:::row
Thassa's Oracle
:::

The trigger checks the devotion on resolution, so if you have cards left in your
library and your opponent has removal for the Oracle, you will have 2 fewer
devotion to {U} and won't win the game as a result. Watch out for effects that
restock cards into your library such as :card[Endurance] and :card[Terminus].

### Veil of Summer

:::row
VoS
:::

It prevents mill effects from cards like :card[Thought Scour], :card[Hedron
Crab], or :card[Witherbloom Command]. Conversely, it will not stop
:card[Mindbreak Trap], :card[Stifle], or even :card[Surgical Extraction] from
being effective. It will also draw you a card most of the time, so be careful to
keep this in mind if you have 0 cards left in your deck. Sometimes referred to
as the one-mana :card[Cryptic Command] because it usually draws a card and
counters something, Veil also lets you play cards through a :card[Chalice of the
Void].

## Opponent Interactions

This section is not meant to be exhaustive as there are _a lot_ of Magic: the
Gathering cards in the format. Instead we will try to cover a wide range of
different interactions so that you extrapolate on your own and recognize
different effects under the same examples listed.

### Aluren &bull; Show and Tell

:::row
Aluren
Show and Tell
:::

You can use the opponent's :card[Show and Tell] to your advantage with a bit of
planning ahead. For instance by cycling into an oracle if you have a way to draw
into the pile with :card[Show and Tell] on the stack. :card[Aluren] is similar
in that its symmetry will allow you to cast the Oracle on top of your opponent's
win.

### Ashiok, Dream Render &bull; Aven Mindcensor &bull; Opposition Agent

:::row
Ashiok, Dream Render
Aven Mindcensor
Opposition Agent
:::

If there is an :card[Aven Mindcensor] in play, a resolved :card[Doomsday] will
be able to search only the top 4 cards of the library and the entire graveyard.
The replacement effect still doesn't allow you to select fewer than 5 cards for
your pile, unless of course you have no graveyard and are left with only 4 cards
to choose from.

:card[Opposition Agent] works differently in that your opponent will control you
while you search and while doing so they will have access to both your library
and graveyard. The selected 5 cards end up in the agent's exile, while the rest
is exiled normally. Unless Oracle is in your hand, this will most likely result
in your defeat.

### Consign to Memory &bull; Stifle

:::row
Consign to Memory
Stifle
:::

While only the latter can stop :card[Conjurer's Bauble] activations, cyclers,
and fetchlands, those 2 cards can prevent :card[ToA] and :card[EtW] _Storm_
triggers and more importantly, :card[TO] triggers.

There is a common misconception about :card[Misdirection] and other similar
effects being able to redirect a :card[Consign to Memory] onto its own
_Replicate_ trigger. However, while you can technically change the target of the
original spell to target the _Replicate_ trigger, the trigger will still be
above the original spell and will therefore resolve first. This means any copy
of the spell will also resolve before the original ever attempts to counter
anything.

### Day's Undoing &bull; Echo of Eons

:::row
Day's Undoing
Echo of Eons
:::

If playing against a known Echo deck, try to make sure you have at least 8 cards
spread across your hand, library and graveyard to avoid losing to an untimely
draw-7. You should already have a minimum of 6 from the :card[Dd] in your
graveyard and your 5-cards pile so having an extra 2 cards in hand or some
uncracked fetchlands, :card[LP] in play and/or :card[EoA] in hand can easily
help achieve this.

### Disruptor Flute &bull; Pithing Needle

:::row
Disruptor Flute
Pithing Needle
:::

Those effects cannot stop :card[LED], :card[LP], or... :card[Dd].

> Newer players often try naming :card[Dd] due to the strange and deprecated
> wording on the _Weatherlight_ printing since it uses a colon like activated
> abilities do.

However they _will_ prevent cyclers and fetchlands, especially troublesome if
unprepared after you've committed to your pile.

:card[Disruptor Flute] typically names :card[Dd] but its _Flash_ flexibility can
be devastating when it names something your pile relies on (e.g. :card[TO],
:card[JWoM]).

### Flickerwisp

:::row
Flickerwisp
:::

An active :card[Aether Vial] on 3 representing :card[Flickerwisp] threatens both
removal and a :card[Stone Rain]. Accounting for both during your sequencing will
be key.

### Grindstone &bull; Predict &bull; Surgical Extraction &bull; Thought Scour

:::row
Grindstone
Predict
Surgical Extraction
Thought Scour
:::

These cards have the ability to disturb the ordering or contents of your library
at instant speed.

### Phyrexian Revoker

:::row
Phyrexian Revoker
:::

It stops :card[LED] and :card[LP], in addition to the cyclers. It cannot prevent
fetchland or :card[SI] activations.

### Portent

:::row
Portent
:::

It can reorder your library at sorcery speed. Mostly relevant when passing the
turn.

### Runehorn Hellkite

:::row
Runehorn Hellkite
:::

Very difficult to interact with as this requires specific tools like
:card[Stifle], not typically found in lists. Contrary to cards like :card[Ruin
Grinder], :card[Raphael's Technique], or :card[Sail into the West], it is not a
_may_, and the instant speed makes it impossible to sequence around.

### Skyclave Apparition &bull; Solitude

:::row
Skyclave Apparition
Solitude
:::

These are listed together to represent 0-mana removal. White-based fair matchups
can appear inoffensive at first, with seemingly little interaction, but remember
that besides mana denial, they also have access to a lot of instant-speed
removal, sometimes free of mana cost. Be careful when considering a pile with
cards remaining in your library.

### Teferi, Time Raveler

:::row
Teferi, Time Raveler
:::

You cannot cast anything using :card[SI] with Teferi in play. However, you can
use the Isle to play a land.

You also cannot cast spells using a _Miracle_ trigger nor can you cast a spell
off a _Bargain_'d :card[Beseech the Mirror].

### Terminus &bull; Wheel of Sun and Moon

:::row
Terminus
Wheel of Sun and Moon
:::

These cards can put cards back into your pile. The former will make all your
usual ways of digging into or thinning the pile useless (i.e. fetchlands,
cantrips and cyclers won't reduce the size of your pile anymore). While the
latter, cast with the Oracle trigger still on the stack, will reduce your
devotion to {U} by 2 in addition to increasing the size of your library by one.
